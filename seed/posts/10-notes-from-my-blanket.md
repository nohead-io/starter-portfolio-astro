*Noir is a pug. She came to the Copenhagen studio with Elias on the first day in the joinery and has refused to leave since. This post was dictated over several afternoons and typed up by Elias, who has corrected nothing except the spelling.*

I live under the workbench. The workbench does not move, which is the best thing about it. It is warm at one end in the afternoon because of the window, and the people do not go under it because they are too tall. There is a blanket. It is mine.

## On the joinery

The joinery is better than the old place. The old place had stairs and nowhere to lie down. The new place has a wooden floor and a great deal of sawdust, which smells of trees and also, near the kitchen, of cheese.

The sawdust is the best part. It gets into the blanket, and then the blanket smells of trees too. Elias shakes the blanket out on Mondays. I do not know why. I put the sawdust back by Wednesday.

There are eleven racks of wood. Two of them have old boards that nobody is allowed to touch. I am allowed to smell them. I have smelled them a lot. They smell the same every time, which Elias says is the point.

## On Fridays

On Fridays a woman called Birthe comes. She used to own the joinery, or the joinery used to own her, I'm not sure which. She brings a thermos and a paper bag. The paper bag has biscuits in it. She says they are for everyone, but she gives them to me first, so they are mine.

On Fridays the band saw also goes on, which is the worst thing about it. It lives in the back room and makes a sound like a very large bee that is angry about something. I go and lie by the front door until it stops. Then I come back and inspect what it made. It is usually a small piece of wood. It is never a biscuit.

## On the small building

For many Fridays Elias and Birthe made a small building. Birthe calls it a doll's house. It is long and grey and has a roof that goes up and down and up and down, fourteen times. I counted. I can count to fourteen if there are biscuits.

The roof comes off. Then everyone leans over and looks inside, and nobody looks at me. There are very small people in it. They do not smell of anything.

## On the matter of the balsa column

I would like to give my side.

There was a stick on the end of the workbench. It was light and it smelled of nothing much, which is suspicious, so I took it to my blanket to examine it properly. Examining it took some time. When Elias found it, it was shorter. That is what examining does.

Elias made a noise. Birthe laughed, made a new stick in no time at all, and said I had good taste. Birthe is the most sensible person in the joinery.

Then the small building went into a box and away on an aeroplane with Elias. I was not consulted.

## On the people

There are three people here most days, and a fourth on Fridays.

- **Elias** is mine. He brings me in the morning and takes me home at night. He draws at the desk by the window, and when he is thinking he scratches my ears without looking.
- **Leela** has small grey squares on her desk. They are not food. I have checked all of them. She says one of them is a copy of twenty-seven and that it is the important one. It tastes the same as the others.
- **Jens** is new. He arrived and then immediately left again for a place called Dublin. He came back a month later smelling of rain. I have decided to tolerate Jens.
- **Birthe** has biscuits. See above.

The others live in Dublin, which is somewhere you go on an aeroplane, like the small building. They appear on the big screen on Monday mornings. I bark at the screen to say hello. Callum always says "hello, Noir" back, so Callum is all right.

## On visitors

People come to the joinery with parcels of balsa and card, and a man comes every morning with the post. I meet all of them at the door. Most of them say I am small. I am exactly the size that fits under a workbench.

## On being an author

Elias says I am called Noir because it is French for black, and I am the blackest thing in the studio, blacker than the band saw. I am not French. I am from Valby.

He also says that everything in this journal was done by someone, and that each post says who. I would like it noted that I have inspected every delivery to the joinery, supervised every Friday, and examined one column. Nobody had written that down until now.

There. Now it is written down.
