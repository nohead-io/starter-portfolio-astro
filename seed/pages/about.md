## What we do

We design buildings, and we repair them. Some are new: a shelter at a viewpoint, a waiting room, flats in an old foundry. Many are old and loved and leaking, and the job is to keep what people love about them while making them work again.

We care most about what happens after the architects go home. Where people stand while they wait, which way they turn in the wind, whether they sit on the bench or lean on the wall beside it. When we got those details right on the Accordion, the complaints a month later were about the buses, not the building.

## How we work

On every project so far, the same people have done the whole job, from the first visit to the day the doors opened.

1. **Watching.** Before we draw anything, we spend time where the building is: who uses it, how long they stay, where they stand. One observation per index card.
2. **Survey.** For an old building, we start with what is already known: the original drawings, the records, every crack, and whatever a neighbour remembers.
3. **Drawing.** Plans, models and, first of all, the long section, cellar to roof, because it shows how every part meets the next. If it doesn't work there, it doesn't work.
4. **Site.** On the Accordion we stayed on site until it was finished, and went back a month later to see what people did with it.

## Where we are

**Dublin**, since 2019: the first floor of a Georgian house, with very tall windows, a kettle that is never cold, and a bench on the half landing where clients wait.

**Copenhagen**, since 2022, and since April 2026 in a former joinery that closed in 2019. The timber racks are now shelves, the band saw still cuts, and we run our workshops at the long table in the front room.
