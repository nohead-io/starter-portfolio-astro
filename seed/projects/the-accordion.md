Fennell Quay Bus Station opened in 1964, by the river, under a roof of fourteen folds of thin concrete shell, 1,120 square metres of it. Dublin calls it the Accordion, among five other names. By 2026 the folds had leaked for thirty years, the clock had stopped in 1997, and the Quays Bus Company, which runs the station, had a steel canopy drawn to hang under the roof and catch the water. Ríona Kelly, its head of estates, asked for one more opinion first.

## What we did

- **Survey.** We read the building before drawing on it: the Modern Register's card for it, with twenty-two years of a volunteer's pencil notes on the back, the original drawings, and every fold, from a cherry picker.
- **Conservation.** A one-page schedule of what stays, what is repaired and what goes. The roof, the terrazzo floor, the clock and the timetable boards stayed. The 1981 kiosk went, and uncovered the foundation stone, hidden for forty-five years. Forty-one sample patches on the north fold before one matched the 1964 concrete; every fold's valley resealed, and the downpipes inside the columns cleared of fifty-eight years of pigeon.
- **Design.** A glass waiting room under the deepest fold, where people already stood out of the wind, with oak benches facing away from it and the foundation stone on its back wall. Warm, and lit in the evening so the roof glows from the quay.
- **Landscape.** The forecourt between the concourse and the river wall: granite paving along the line of the folds, a low wall to sit on with its back to the river, and four alders to break the wind.
- **Planning.** The station is a protected structure, so removing the kiosk and adding the waiting room needed permission. The application went in at the start of May, with a conservation report, and permission came on 1 July.
- **Construction.** Seven months on site with the station open, one bay at a time, the contractor working from the one-page schedule.

## What changed

The roof doesn't leak. The station reopened on Open House weekend and 3,100 people came through the doors; at six o'clock on the last Tuesday in October, the waiting room was full. The clock is right twice a day and every other minute too. The journal has the whole story, mistakes included.
