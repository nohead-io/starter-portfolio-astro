The Coast Road runs 180 kilometres around two bays in the west, from one small town to another, past cliffs, beaches, a lighthouse and nine viewpoints with lay-bys just big enough for a camper van. Two Bays Tourism, a team of four, wanted somewhere for people to stop at each one. What people did at the viewpoints, in March, was stand with their backs to the wind and their hoods up, looking at the view over their shoulder.

## What we did

- **Landscape.** Aoife spent four days at the viewpoints in March, over two visits, with 230 index cards, writing down where people stood, how long they stayed and which way they turned. Almost everyone turned their back to the weather first and the view second. Then she sited each shelter, and the gravel path to it from the lay-by.
- **Design.** One shelter, nine times, one of them longer: a larch frame, a bench, a roof that slopes down towards the prevailing wind, and a single opening cut where the view is. Each is turned to its own view and its own weather, so no two face the same way.
- **Planning.** Nine planning applications in two counties, with the same drawings and nine site plans.
- **Construction.** Built in a boatyard in the second bay, in sections small enough for a trailer, and bolted down in a week.

## What changed

Most stops at a viewpoint now end on the bench. The shelters are used most between the fourth and the seventh viewpoints, where the wind is worst and the views are best, and the boatyard has built two more for a harbour further north.
