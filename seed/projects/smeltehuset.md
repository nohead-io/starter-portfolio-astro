Smeltehuset was a brass foundry from 1912 to 1987, then a warehouse, then empty. A cooperative of twenty-four households bought it in 2021 to live in. The obvious plan, and the one the cooperative was shown first, filled the tall casting hall with three floors of flats and knocked the chimney down to make room for parking.

## What we did

- **Feasibility.** Three options, drawn as long sections so the cooperative could see the hall's height in each: the flats fit in the two lower wings and the old offices, with room to spare.
- **Design.** Twenty-four flats, from one room to five, around a courtyard, and the casting hall kept whole: heated, with power, water and the crane rail left where it was, for the cooperative to make into the workshop it needed. The chimney is braced inside, with a light at the top the residents switch on when the hall is in use.
- **Planning.** The plan the cooperative was shown first had the chimney down for parking. We made the case to the municipality for keeping it, as the last sign of the foundry on the street, with bicycle parking instead of cars, and permission came with the chimney in it.
- **Construction.** Before the builders came, the residents stripped out the old offices themselves, on weekends, and cleaned and kept the bricks for the courtyard.

## What changed

The flats are lived in and well liked. The casting hall took longer: for two years it held forty bicycles, a ping-pong table and a great many boxes. In October 2026 twelve of the residents came to our long table in Copenhagen with 163 index cards and decided what it is for, and the journal has how that went.
